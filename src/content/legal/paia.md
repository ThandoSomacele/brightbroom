## 1. About this manual

It explains what records we keep and how you can ask for access to them. One manual covers both businesses, because the same company keeps their records. The manual is free.

- If a breach puts personal information at risk, we tell the people affected and the Information Regulator, as section 22 of POPIA requires.

## 8. How to ask for a record

- Fill in Form 2 of the PAIA Regulations (https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/InfoRegSA-PAIA-Form02-Reg7.pdf) and email it to our Information Officer, or deliver it to our address (section 2).
- Include proof of your identity. If you ask for someone else, include proof that you may act for them.
- Describe the record well enough for us to find it, say how you want access (for example a copy, an electronic copy or to look at it), and how we should tell you our decision.
- Say which right you want to exercise or protect, and why you need the record for it (section 53 of PAIA).

We decide within 30 days of receiving your request. We may extend this once, by up to 30 days, and will tell you why (section 57). If we do not answer in time, the law treats your request as refused (section 58). If a record cannot be found or does not exist, we will tell you so under oath (section 55).

To ask for your own personal information, you can also simply email us at {{brand.supportEmail}}. We may ask you to complete Form 2 so we have what we need, but you do not pay the request fee.

If you need help to make a request, contact the Information Officer.

## 10. When we may refuse access

PAIA lets us refuse access only on the grounds in sections 63 to 69, for example to protect:

- another person's privacy (section 63);
- another company's commercial information (section 64);
- information someone gave us in confidence (section 65);
- the safety of people or property (section 66);
- records privileged from production in legal proceedings (section 67);
- our own commercial information, such as trade secrets (section 68);
- research by us or someone else (section 69).

We must still give access if the record would reveal a serious breach of the law or an imminent and serious risk to public safety or the environment, and the public interest in it clearly outweighs the harm (section 70). If we refuse, we give our reasons.

## 11. If you disagree with our decision

A private company has no internal appeal. If we refuse your request, or you disagree with a fee or an extension, you may, within 180 days:

- complain to the Information Regulator on Form 5 (PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za); or
- apply to court.
